# Teaching principles — ⬜ NOT STARTED

Every lesson follows the same pattern:

1. **Question** – a motivating puzzle.
2. **Picture** – an interactive visual.
3. **Experiment** – learner manipulates the system.
4. **Pattern** – the learner observes regularities.
5. **Mathematics** – introduce notation only after intuition.
6. **Summary** – connect the visual to the formal concept.
7. **Sandbox** – free exploration.

# Success criteria — ⬜ NOT STARTED

A learner who completes the guided lesson should be able to:

* Explain the difference between a classical bit and a qubit.
* Interpret a qubit as a state on the Bloch sphere.
* Understand how measurement changes a quantum state.
* Describe what the Pauli operators do geometrically and algebraically.
* Explain why quantum gates are unitary and reversible.
* Construct simple two-qubit states using tensor products.
* Recognise the distinction between separable and entangled states.
* Explain what Bell states represent and why they exhibit non-classical correlations.
* Read introductory quantum-computing material without the notation feeling unfamiliar.

The educational goal should mirror QuasiShor's: not to train students to build quantum hardware or derive every proof, but to leave them with an accurate mental model of the foundational mathematics that makes quantum algorithms understandable.
